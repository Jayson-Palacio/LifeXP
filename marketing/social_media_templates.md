## 🎨 Adobe Creative Suite Production Cues
Use these guidelines in **Premiere Pro**, **After Effects**, and **Photoshop** to ensure videos match Kaeluma's premium aesthetic:
* **Typography**: Use **Outfit Bold** or **Inter ExtraBold** for on-screen captions. Position captions in the center-bottom "safe zone" (avoid covering with social UI elements).
* **Colors**: Accent keywords with Kaeluma Gold (`#F59E0B`) or Glowing Purple (`#A855F7`) on a clean white base text.
* **Audio Design**: Layer in a distinct metallic "Coin Clink" sound effect every time a checkmark is clicked, and a nostalgic 16-bit "Level Up" sound effect for progress moments.
* **Editing Style**: Fast-paced jump-cuts on speech pauses. Use brief zoom-ins (110%) on key phrases to maintain visual interest.

### Template B: r/ADHD_parents (Neurodiversity focus, zero pitch)
**Title**: We ended our morning routine battles with an ad-free visual "dopamine loop." Sharing what worked.

> Hey everyone,
>
> Our son (7, ADHD combined type) has always struggled with daily transitions. We tried stickers, whiteboards, and chore charts. They worked for a week before the novelty wore off.
>
> As a developer, I spent some time researching why those failed. ADHD brains struggle with delayed gratification—they need immediate feedback loops.
>
> I built a system that treats routines like an RPG quest board. Here's why it has kept him motivated for six months:
>
> * **Immediate Feedback**: Tapping a mission plays a metal coin clink sound and fills an XP bar instantly. It provides dopamine *in the moment*, not at the end of the week.
> * **Visual Progression**: He unlocks custom dashboard themes and animated avatar rings as he levels up, giving him a sense of digital progression.
> * **Balanced Economy**: He earns coins to spend on things like screen time or picking dinner. It teaches him the value of his efforts.
>
> I made the app public and completely free (no paywalls, no ads, just a voluntary donation link in settings) because I know how hard it is to find tools that actually help neurodivergent kids. It's called Kaeluma (kaeluma.com).
>
> Even if you build this manually on a whiteboard, the trick is making the reward *immediate* and *visual*. 
>
> What routine strategies have actually stuck for your kids?
